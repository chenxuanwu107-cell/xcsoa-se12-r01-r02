# B — Two sharing and contribution options; neither selected

**CUSTOM_LICENSE / SOURCE_AVAILABLE / LEGAL_REVIEW_REQUIRED**.
Both options preserve individual commercial work, paid independent customer
projects and ordinary output rights. Neither grants an enterprise customer
Skill operation, deployment, API, organizational integration or proxy use.
The proposed license must select exactly one option before adoption.

## Option S1 — No additional redistribution permission

Proposed operative text for Section 5:

> Eligible Individuals may privately modify the Skill for permitted uses. Except
> for applicable platform rights and mandatory law, no additional right is granted
> to share or redistribute the Skill or protected modifications, commercially or
> noncommercially. No contribution-submission or maintainer-relicensing right is
> created by this option. A separately lawful contribution route and contributor
> permission are required before incorporating contributed material.

Review comments and independently written ideas can be submitted only within
their own rights and platform terms; this option does not grant a copyright
license over an author's new patch or authorize posting protected Skill text.
Existing platform rights are not withdrawn. This is the restrictive option,
not a way to disable a public GitHub repository's native fork rights.

## Option S2 — Same-license noncommercial individual sharing and contributions

Proposed operative text for Section 5:

> Eligible Individuals may noncommercially share the Skill and their protected
> modifications with other eligible natural persons under this exact adopted
> license, preserving its text, attribution, provenance and applicable notices.
> Identify modifications and do not claim inherited validation or endorsement.
> Do not charge for, commercially host, white-label, sublicense on different
> terms, or offer operational access to the Skill. Recipients obtain only the
> same eligible-use rights; no organizational operational right is created.
> This restriction on sharing the Skill does not restrict earning income from
> permitted independent professional services or delivering ordinary outputs.
>
> An eligible Individual may submit a contribution for inclusion, provided that
> the Individual identifies their own protected expression, AI assistance and
> third-party components, and has the authority to grant the submission rights.
> For the rights actually held in that contribution, the contributor grants the
> verified project rights holder a non-exclusive, worldwide, royalty-free right
> to review, reproduce, modify, incorporate and distribute it as part of the Skill
> under this same adopted license. This is not an assignment, an enterprise
> operational license, or permission to relicense under different terms. No
> authority to grant platform rights over others' content is presumed.

Public pages remain readable by organizations; that unavoidable/public reading
does not make an organization an eligible operational recipient. Public hosting
can impose broader rights regardless of recipient wording. Every platform grant
must therefore be cleared separately. Sending code to a corporate maintainer
for copyright review/inclusion is not a grant to operate the whole Skill for
that maintainer's business; counsel must confirm this narrow contribution role.

## Fork and PR comparison

| Mechanism | S1 | S2 | Shared boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Private individual modifications | Expressly allowed | Expressly allowed | Does not authorize enterprise operation. |
| Same-license noncommercial sharing outside GitHub | No extra permission | Expressly proposed for eligible natural persons | S2 still needs actual source rights; downstream rights cannot be invented. |
| GitHub native Fork of lawfully hosted public content | Platform rights remain; cannot promise to prohibit Fork | Platform rights remain; S2 adds a limited project sharing right | Fork is not proof of unrestricted operational rights; D.4/D.5 and actual terms must be reviewed. |
| Pull request with protected Skill modifications | No additional project permission or contributor grant; establish another lawful route first | Explicit proposed contribution route under the same adopted terms | D.6 repository inbound terms and D.4/D.5 platform rights are separate; “a PR exists” does not prove chain of title. |
| Corporate employee submits employer-owned code | No assumption of permission | Not automatically an eligible natural-person contribution | Needs the actual right holder's authority; do not treat a personal account as an assignment. |
| Project recipient redistributes contributions | Separate clear contributor permission required | Proposed narrow inclusion/distribution grant, without broader relicensing | Do not merge any contribution with unresolved rights. |
| GitHub introduction-only path | No Skill/patch in its Issues, PRs, attachments or Actions | Same; S2 sharing/contributions would use a separately cleared channel | A full Skill PR would defeat the introduction-only boundary. |

The table is a compatibility analysis, not a finding that either option satisfies
every GitHub-required grant. See [platform paths](PLATFORM_PATHS.md). Full-text
hosting and external same-license sharing remain separate decisions. No CLA,
copyright assignment, blanket sublicense or automatic contribution acceptance
has been created in this revision.
